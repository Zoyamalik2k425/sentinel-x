# Limitations and Responsible-Use Boundaries

## Synthetic evidence

All data and labels are generated. Injected scenarios encode design assumptions and are not confirmed financial crime, gambling harm, or representative prevalence. Results vary with seed, population, event volume, visibility, and review budget. No metric should be generalized to OLG players or operations.

## Data and identity

Channel schemas are fictional and intentionally unequal. Land-based activity is incomplete and sometimes unlinked. Probable identity evidence is not a confirmed match. Missing activity can suppress a score and is not evidence of safety. Synthetic deduplication cannot cover every real reconciliation problem.

## Models and thresholds

Anomaly severity and risk indices are rankings, not calibrated probabilities. Isolation Forest and robust change methods can flag legitimate novelty and miss subtle changes. Peer groups depend on observed behavior. Network evidence can reflect legitimate shared households or instruments. The fixed threshold of 60 was ineffective for several models in the documented run and requires development-set calibration.

## Fairness

Age band and region are excluded from direct scoring and used for outcome auditing only. Synthetic group sizes and positive counts are small, disparity ratios are unstable, and equal flag rates do not establish fairness. A real assessment would require representative data, label-quality analysis, intersectional testing, uncertainty intervals, and review of downstream decisions.

## Investigation workflow

Generated narratives summarize supplied evidence only. Alternative explanations are hypotheses, not facts. The local session store is temporary, unauthenticated, and unsuitable for regulated records. Recommendations support human judgment and never authorize account freezes, gambling restrictions, player contact, or law-enforcement reporting.

## Production gaps

The prototype has no production ingestion, authentication, role-based access, encryption/key management, retention policy, data lineage service, model registry, monitoring, incident response, accessibility certification, privacy impact assessment, or independent validation. Any pilot requires authorized stakeholders and formal legal, privacy, security, responsible-gambling, AML, and model-risk review.
